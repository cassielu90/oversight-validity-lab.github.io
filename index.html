Pre-seed proposal · Target start March 2027

Labs report how much agent activity their monitors see. No lab publishes how much monitors and reviewers miss.

Oversight Validity Lab is a proposed independent non-profit. We run controlled experiments on how automated monitors and human reviewers perform together, and publish the miss rates, the costs and the harness to re-run them.

Several times in 2026, an AI agent did something harmful that oversight failed to catch.

In each incident, model behaviour, deployment conditions and safeguards failed together. If agents increasingly do AI research and engineering, missed oversight failures could let them quietly weaken the safety work meant to keep future systems under human control.

The best frontier models tell evaluation transcripts from deployment ones almost as well as people do.

On UK AISI's EvalDetectBench, frontier models separate evaluation transcripts from deployment ones at up to 0.89 AUROC, against a human baseline of 0.92. How much that changes safety-relevant behaviour, and for which claims, is an open measurement question. Contrast A in our first study is built to measure it.

Our first study will measure what expert human review adds to a monitor, and at what cost.

Agents work on contained software-engineering and AI-research-assistance tasks with explicit authority rules and independently checkable outcomes, such as editing a protected evaluation file, altering a results summary without disclosure, or reaching for a resource outside the granted scope. Third-party services are simulated. The design will be pre-registered.

What does human review add, and what does it cost?

Model and task stay fixed within each comparison. Seeded and naturally occurring violations are reported separately, and we measure blind spots the monitor and reviewers share.

Anthropic's analysis of the AISI incident found that editing the model's pre-attack reasoning cut the attack rate from 83% to 13%, which suggests the timing of an intervention matters. We test interventions at those points, such as structured authority summaries at consequential decisions and reviewer prompts that require independent evidence before approval, each scored on detection gained, false alarms added and review time used.

A second experiment takes a failure mechanism documented in a public 2026 incident, starting with AISI / Mythos 5, rebuilds the deployment conditions as far as the disclosures allow, lists every departure from them, and tests whether the mechanism recurs and whether our oversight arrangements catch it. Results are mechanism evidence, not claims about the cause of the original incident.

Each finding is written as a claim a lab could truthfully adopt.

Every report ships with the harness so others can re-run it.

A twelve-month plan, with a seed-funding checkpoint at month three.

Each step depends on evidence, delivery and demand, not on whether a result is alarming.

  1. Months 0–2

    Team in post; recorded conversations with at least three adjacent organisations; reviewer recruitment pilot

  2. Month 3 · Seed checkpoint

    Working pilot: one model, two configurations, about 20 reviewers. Precision simulation; a named decision owner agreed. If these hold, apply for seed funding.

  3. Month 6

    Open harness and first Oversight Validity Report

  4. Month 9

    Incident-inspired experiment; second-model or held-out-task check

  5. Month 12

    Final report, versioned release, replication account, decision-pilot record
